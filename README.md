# FASENS_Messungen

## FASENS project 
FrequenzAnaloge SENSoren: was a successful German BMFT-funded (BMBF predecessor) collaborative R&D project in the early 1990s focused on resonant (frequency-analog) microsensors using silicon micromachining. 

### Project Goals and Scope
- Core Principle: Sensors where the measured quantity (pressure, force, flow, acceleration, etc.) shifts the resonance frequency of a vibrating microstructure (beams or diaphragms). This provides a quasi-digital, noise-robust output signal with high sensitivity and inherent self-checking capabilities.
- Excitation Methods: Piezoelectric thin films (ZnO), PZT ceramics, quartz technology, or electrothermal/electrostatic drives.
- Materials: Primarily silicon microstructures with deposited piezoelectric layers for actuation and sensing.
- Partners: Hahn-Schickard Institute for Micro- and Information Technology (IMIT, Villingen-Schwenningen), plus industry partners like Bosch, MotoMeter, GMS, and Bizerba.
Duration & Scale: ~1989–1992/93, total budget ~5.6 million DM. Concluded with a well-attended public presentation in March 1993. 

### Measurement Protocols
- Optical vibration measurements: Polytec Laser Vibrometer (e.g., OFV-1102 HR) for non-contact characterization of resonance modes, frequencies, and quality factors on tiny structures (down to tens of µm).
- Electrical characterization: HP 4194A (now Keysight) Impedance/Gain-Phase Analyzer for piezoelectric drive and impedance analysis.
- Additional tools: Spectrum analyzers (e.g., HP 3588A), custom fixtures for applying pressure/force/flow/temperature, xy-stages for precise laser positioning, and FEM modeling (ANSYS, etc.) for design optimization. 

These setups were crucial for experimental verification of the resonant behavior, modal analysis, Q-factor determination, and validation against simulations. Optical methods were especially important early on because electrical readout of the tiny structures was challenging. 

### Outcomes and Impact
- Prototypes of resonant pressure, force, and flow sensors.
- Demonstrated advantages: high sensitivity, low hysteresis, batch fabrication potential, good long-term stability (especially with quartz).
- Publications and FEM modeling work on beam-on-diaphragm structures, mode coupling, piezoelectric coupling factors, etc.
- Contributed to microsystem technology (MST) transfer to industry.
